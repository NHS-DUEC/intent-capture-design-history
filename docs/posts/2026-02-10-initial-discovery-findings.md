## What we did

In January 2026 we ran a discovery phase to understand how people find and access NHS services online. We spoke to 18 participants across a range of ages, locations and digital confidence levels.

We also reviewed analytics from NHS.UK to understand where people were dropping off during common journeys, such as finding a GP or booking a vaccination.

## What we found

### People struggle to know where to start

Many participants described feeling overwhelmed by the number of options on the NHS.UK homepage. Several used Google rather than the NHS website directly, finding it quicker to search for "NHS book GP appointment" than to navigate from the homepage.

> "I never really know where to click first. I usually just Google it."
> — Participant, age 54

### The difference between 'Find' and 'Book' is unclear

Participants frequently confused links that find a service (for example, "Find a GP") with links that directly complete a task ("Book a GP appointment"). This led to frustration when they landed on a results page rather than a booking flow.

### Low digital confidence users rely on signposting from staff

Participants with lower digital confidence said they typically only visited NHS.UK after being directed there by a receptionist or clinician. They were unlikely to browse independently.

## What this means for our design

These findings suggest we should focus on:

- Reducing the number of decisions a user needs to make upfront
- Making the distinction between finding and booking clearer
- Ensuring the most common journeys are reachable in as few steps as possible

We will carry these insights into alpha.
