## What we built

Based on our discovery findings we built a prototype that introduced a task-led homepage structure. Rather than organising content by service type (for example, "GP services", "Hospital services"), we grouped content around what people are trying to do:

- Get medical help now
- Manage a long-term condition
- Book or check an appointment
- Find a service near me

We tested this with 12 participants over two rounds of moderated usability testing in March 2026.

## What worked well

### Task-led groupings reduced time on task

On average, participants found their destination 40% faster using the task-led prototype compared to a baseline test on the current NHS.UK homepage. This was particularly pronounced for participants over 60.

### "Get medical help now" performed strongly

This grouping was understood immediately by nearly all participants. Several said it was the first thing they looked for when feeling unwell.

## What did not work

### "Manage a long-term condition" caused confusion

Participants with chronic conditions did not identify with this label. One participant with diabetes said:

> "I don't really think of myself as having a 'long-term condition'. I just want to order my repeat prescription."

We will rework this grouping to focus on specific tasks (for example, "Order a repeat prescription", "Check your test results") rather than a broad category.

### The search bar was being ignored

Eye-tracking data showed most participants scanned the grouped links rather than reaching for the search bar, even when a direct search would have been faster. We will explore making search more prominent in the next iteration.

## Next steps

- Redesign the "Manage a long-term condition" grouping
- Run an A/B test on search bar placement
- Begin work on the mobile navigation pattern
