This design history explains how the AI Intent Capture work is evolving as part of wider access, triage and navigation thinking across the NHS.

The work is concerned with a difficult design problem: people often arrive with a need, worry, symptom or question, but do not necessarily know which NHS service, route or pathway is appropriate. The aim is to help people express what they need in their own words, and to use that understanding to support safer, clearer routes to care.

## How to read this design history

This repository has three layers:

1. **Chronological posts and timeline** describe how the work has changed over time.
2. **Reference pages** define stable concepts, principles, architecture and terminology.
3. **Decision records, claims and assumptions** explain important choices, evidence gaps and consequences.

Some content records agreed decisions. Some records working assumptions or hypotheses. Where something is not yet settled, the page should say so explicitly.

## Current editorial position

The design history should make the team's thinking visible. It should record not only what was designed, but why particular options were explored, challenged, accepted, changed or rejected.
